Chapter 7, Measuring domestic output and national income

Nominal GDP versus real GDP

An economy makes only pizza. Nominal GDP uses each year's prices, so it rises when prices rise even if output does not. Real GDP uses base-year prices (Year 1), so it only rises when output rises. The table fills in Years 1 to 3. Work out Years 4 and 5 yourself and click Check.

Nominal GDPReal GDP
YearUnitsPricePrice indexNominalReal
Price index = (this year's price ÷ base-year price) × 100. Real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ (price index ÷ 100).

Chapter 13, Fiscal policy, deficits, and debt

Close the gap with fiscal policy

Pick a starting point. In a recession, real GDP sits below full employment. With demand-pull inflation, it sits above. Then change government spending (G) or taxes and watch AD shift and the economy move along AS, the short-run aggregate supply curve. Every dollar of new spending moves AD by the multiplier, so a small policy change goes a long way. Prices are sticky downward here, as in the textbook, so cutting AD lowers output but not the price level.

Chapter 14, Money, the Federal Reserve, and interest rates

How banks create money

Someone deposits new cash in Bank A. The bank keeps a share as reserves and lends out the rest. The borrower spends the loan, and the seller deposits it in Bank B, which does the same thing. The cash deposit itself does not change the money supply, because cash just became a checking deposit. The loans are the new money. Since 2020 the Fed has required no reserves. Banks still keep some, and 1/R shows the most lending a given reserve share allows. Click Next bank to follow the chain.
