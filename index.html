Chapter 13, Fiscal policy, deficits, and debt

Close the gap with fiscal policy

Pick a starting point. In a recession, real GDP sits below full employment. With demand-pull inflation, it sits above. Then change government spending (G) or taxes and watch AD shift. Every dollar of new spending moves AD by the multiplier, so a small policy change goes a long way. Prices are sticky downward here, as in the textbook, so cutting AD lowers output but not the price level.

Chapter 14, Money, the Federal Reserve, and interest rates

How banks create money

Someone deposits new cash in Bank A. The bank keeps the required share as reserves and lends out the rest. The borrower spends the loan, and the seller deposits it in Bank B, which does the same thing. The cash deposit itself does not change the money supply, because cash just became a checking deposit. The loans are the new money. Click Next bank to follow the chain.

Chapter 14, Money, the Federal Reserve, and interest rates

The money market and bond prices

The total demand for money (Dm) adds two parts. The transactions demand (Dt) depends on nominal GDP. The asset demand (Da) falls as the interest rate rises, because holding cash means giving up interest. The Fed sets the money supply (Sm). Shift either side and watch the interest rate and the price of a bond that pays a fixed $50 a year.
